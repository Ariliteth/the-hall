Chapter 1: The Overfull Beginning

Dry heels cracked between the same two floorboards each morning before breakfast woke up. A gentle path past not enough clutter to delay the morning always lead straight to coffee. Straight in the way that the steps haven't been taken but the intent has, or the coffee is tasted before the first drop of water, itself, wakes up. Not a thing was different this morning. Not of her steps, not the floorboards, did she think. But this was a passage, her passage, through the doorway into another day. Another day that started the same, but may not be.

Countertop nudged her hip in the way bossy laminate must keep its waitresses in tempo. She opened to the cold air of her fridge knowing her craving wouldn't be found in anything but the bottom of her blue mug. She sighed and looked at it all anyway. With a turn and a thump that might suggest air got the wind knocked out of it, she approached the altar to some Barista's Finest. Finally, though right on time.

Sharpness bit somewhere below her knee and, watched by a device hosting water's favorite way to be boiled, she gasped, winced, and looked down. There was nothing there but what she knew was attached, and what she knew was unclothed. It must be the bite of some muscle she hasn't worked out enough.

The right sequence occurred and gentle aroma heralded coffee's yawn. Perhaps there was enough time to get the paper, and her feet already knew they'd greet her welcome mat on the way through a door none made ice for. Outside, a cool air waved, the only neighbor finding warmth in what would be oversight to the others. The paper was sometimes here but today it was just there. Slightly damp, without need for answers, without interrupting whatever was Breaking yesterday.

She would've gathered a breath that mixed Some Barista's Finest with the farm down the road if she had not turned back inside at the precise moment she did. She loved cows without the smell of cow, even without having truly met one.

A shower would betray how busy she has been.

Sitting down now would do the same, but guiltier.

Back towards the fridge that was now ready, her hands offered her eyes a moment to glaze over while she gathered eggs or butter or pass by whatever label her fruit squeezings chose this year. The rest was simple, and there may be disruptions, but they were not bright. The newspaper sagged longingly towards the bin cradling its brethren. It sighed with her, quieter, sharing the feeling that the words would not feel good.

All were happy when she cupped her mug closer to her face. No news, only aroma that is closer now. To predict how many secrets the paper would keep was almost play for most of the chairs, the sink's pots, and every floor tile on the way. Nearly dropped off to the bin but—

The glass of her window shrieked in worry that it knows what touch feels like now. A tap, a scratch, but certainly that glass. She stopped and quickly looked towards the window. One hand was warm with condensation, the other damp with paper. And then there was a squirrel, not tapping but chewing. She locked with its gaze and for a moment wasn't sure who was caught.

As if recognizing what challenge could be, the bushy tail of the squirrel rocketed its fluff upward to hide the creature's escape. She immediately, as if pulled by some acorn-shaped gravity, hurried towards the window. She didn't worry how much coffee leapt with the hope of tasting Floor or if the other papers were patient enough to wait. She needed this.

It was a window. Well, a window and a squirrel outside doing exceedingly normal things. Something there felt it, but it really wasn't anything particular. Her at the window, squirrel outside the window, and the window. All of it was normal and something didn't feel wrong, but it felt.

Maybe her eggs were ready, or the roads knew she was on the brink of being a few minutes late, but she turned away. Coffee relaxed into her cup while the newspaper sagged the whole way to the bin.

Forgetting her table was for dining, she sank into her couch. Her mug kissed its coaster, leaving a wet ring of its personally blended lipstick below. Steam pirouetted above, proud to be the smelliest of all vapor around. A modest TV screen winked its gaze at them all, seeing everything precisely without all the details. She didn't. She looked back at the window but from a more comfortable space and without the squirrel.

Looking through the window at that angle could almost see ice. Not the kind made for doors and drinks, or that encourages a coat or light vest. The kind that builds sculptures and memories of skating. She distinctly felt her eyes and thanked them, comparing how wet they were to how that particular ice might feel the second after it decides to melt. It was, however, brief in the way talking to a customer too long while working your first day at an old job is.

It was time for coffee. It was also time for her lips to question why the liquid was no longer lukewarm, or her nostrils to query where the vapor had gone.

She must be late for work.

These things will all be here tomorrow, but a second later than six minutes early for her job will be noticed. Different in ways she doesn't know and won't think to perceive.

Chapter 2: Learning What Matters

It is never the jingle of a bell, the hum of a crowd that can be drowned in, or the frantic hair stylings of the previous shift that forecast the workday. It could be, but they are all determined by the only set of follicles that matter but refuse to remain until the end of the workday. That is fine and predictable the same way you shouldn't warm an old coin too much. Which can also be explained but doesn't predict the day.

It is unusual, however, to see one particular booth or desk sit both empty and clean. It is an absence eyebrows raise to fill, but only to prove they are first. There is a flow to follow now and she was almost precisely on time, so there is none to waste. Words are captured by her initiative, or they're already ahead. She doesn't need them to begin anyway.

"Coffee?"

She would shake her head. She likes it just fine, but prefers it certainly and something different now. And the question was from someone else to a patron. Either way, the booth creaked warmly.

Before she ordered, a man entered and uncertainly placed himself. Her order was the usual and mostly a formality. His was disorder, but it became less permanent when his coat was off. Perhaps a hidden inside pocket kept too sharp a secret. The color on her nails loved to feel each lacquered chip on the table as a handshake to their impermanence. Speed wasn't usually part of her order but it was today and a smile actually accompanied it.

So did coffee.

Her eyes were as confused as the grunt she must've made at seeing its heated, dental-ad white enamel begin to steam up her lacquer. The waitress shrugged while her hair tousled. Her nude or overworked nails pointed somehow longly towards the man who she only identified by the freshness of the snow in his hair. The rest of his face was shielded by a hand deliberately intervening yet somehow highlighting whatever this was.

"Send a coffee that way, he said. I ain't one to question."

Maybe the three stared or laughed or obliterated from sheer embarrassment that something could be so out of place. But the moment passed and so did the waitress' attention to someone who wouldn't give her the exact same, inflation adjusted tip.

She had to get up and she did and she went straight through the door that must've just welcomed her, bells in reverse this time. What left her then was something alongside a yoga breath, just completely bereft of actual release or relief. Her neck beckoned the rest to journey to her booth but only her head followed, and only then did she see nothing but the brick in between. That, and the overwhelming sense that her decision may have been the worst of the ones she would have imagined now.

Jingle.

"John. Sorry."

Those weren't names, just icebreakers without manilla. Their eyes were magnets that were each too polite to choose the direction they'd both spin with proximity. His name did not live up to the emotion that crashed around him.

"I didn't mean, well, I didn't mean anything really. I actually was trying to tell her to get me a copy."

He pointed at her food and she nearly barked in protection, but realized he wasn't a threat.

"It looked like pancakes but better. And, yeah, I can see you're not really into this."

She wasn't because she had absolutely no clue what he was talking about through nearly any of those words. She only knew those were her pancakes, ordered in a language between her and these specific walls. She offered to be friendly.

He offered a crisp twenty. The bill, not anything that would make sense here or now.

"Thanks. Take this for your special. Maybe you can teach me how to order it properly next—" condensation wanted to form, "Nevermind."

Remarkable that a coat and mumbling created grenades, and even more that someone could ever forget. Of course the bill was soggy. Tailbones and fibrously-ripped cushions act like real magnets. A vortex of mis-mumbled and insincere byes warped her back to her exact spot.

She didn't know where he sat afterwords. Whether or not it was his previous spot is entirely inconsequential. Her fork missed the mapled fluff a lot today, and the once-white plate screeched each time. The coffee was half there, and half appreciated, by the time she left exact change plus exactly more tip.

Jangle.

Chapter 3: Fragile Waking

She sat there on the floor, lotioned heels pressing sole to the floor but beneath the table.

He entered, but not as a lover or an enigma, but a presence.

And within that presence is you.

Not you, the reader, or you the character.

But you.

It is time.
