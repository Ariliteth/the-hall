# EFDP: Animate Mode

**Spec v0.1** — proof entity: Briny Broadswordfish — context: Tending Field

---

## Governing principle

> We just need enough that it seems like we did for how long we notice.

Animation in FPL is not simulation. It is the honest expression of what an entity already is, released into time at the resolution of attention.

---

## What this mode is

EFDP already knows layers, colors, anchor positions, and element identity. Animate Mode adds two things only: a timeline, and a response rule per tagged element. Everything else is already there.

This is not a separate system. It is EFDP operating on the Crank skeleton and letting the full rendition inherit the motion. You animate the bones; the flesh follows.

---

## The element role taxonomy

Established from Briny. These roles generalize to most characters.

| Role | Tag | Behavior | Briny instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | `anchor` | Everything else orients to this. Leads all motion. Moves least, means most. | The bill |
| Body mass | `body` | Semi-rigid. Slow drift. Holds position against an implied current. Follows anchor with lag. | Main torso |
| Character layer | `character` | Tiny movement, enormous meaning. Minimal displacement, maximum read. Never leads. | Brow and eye |
| Release valve | `release` | Moves most freely. Trails body. The thing that breathes when everything else holds. | Striped fin |
| Opinion | `opinion` | Slow, independent. Has its own idea about direction. Lags everything else. | Tail |

---

## The three animation states

### Idle
Holding position against a slow implied current. All elements present, minimal movement. The release valve breathes. The opinion drifts.

### Noticed
Something entered the field. The anchor orients toward it. Body follows with lag. Character layer registers — one small movement. Release valve catches up last.

### Return
Slow decay back to idle. Opinion is last to agree. Nothing snaps. Everything remembers briefly where it was looking.

## Occlusion

Layer order from EFDP is the render order. The element on top shows. Exception: elements with a declared identity (eye, face, character layer) can assert priority or yield based on what they know about themselves. The system does not solve occlusion globally — it defers to tag knowledge locally.

---

## Topology

Border-first deformation. The boundary moves toward the leading anchor; the interior follows as a field pulled by that edge. Shape stays coherent because it is stretching, not dissolving. Rigid elements translate. Semi-rigid elements stretch at the boundary first.

---

## Non-rigid elements

Resolved by the combination of the above. Non-rigid elements are multiple semi-rigid sections at different scales, layered, with coarser levels moving first and finer levels trailing with a delay gradient. Hair, fin rays, cloth: chunks move, then sub-chunks, root leads, tip lags. No strand simulation required.

> **Resolution rule:** Operate at the resolution of attention for the duration of a glance. A painter did not paint every strand of Mona Lisa's hair. We paint enough that it seems like we did.

---

## Keyframe interpolation

Two anchor-map states (keyframe A, keyframe B) define a motion. The delta between tag positions gives the motion vector per element. N intermediate frames are generated by stepping along that vector, scaled by each element's lag coefficient.

| Element | Lag coefficient (placeholder) |
|---|---|
| Anchor | 1.0 |
| Body | ~0.7 |
| Release valve | ~0.5 with trailing decay |
| Opinion | ~0.4 with directional bias |
| Character layer | ~0.15 |

Coefficients are placeholders. Ratios reflect observed character: the bill barely moves; the tail has opinions.

---

## Subtags and the zoom principle

Within any element role, a layer may respond at a different resolution of notice. Example: a `release` element tagged `hair` may contain a `highlight` sublayer — the part the eye catches first. The highlight moves before the hair mass does, because it is what attention actually lands on.

This is not required for Briny v1. It is noted here because it is the seed of something general: attention is zoomable all the way down. Any element can have a layer that leads it, at whatever resolution the moment requires.

## Briny in the Tending Field

The Tending Field already accepts abstract shapes and muted colors. The Crank shape is the animation skeleton. The full-color rendition inherits motion from it.

Briny arrives first in movement — abstract, muted, alive. Full color and shape fill in as the pipeline matures. This is not a workaround. It is Briny becoming more visible over time as the Field recognizes them.

The aquatic motion vocabulary is native. Everything is slightly distorted, slightly slow, slightly uncertain at the edges. That is not a problem to solve. That is Briny's home.

---

## What is not in scope

Full physics. Strand-level simulation. Global occlusion solving. Learned priors from video training data. Complete time modeling.

These are not needed. The Notice bit is the scope.
